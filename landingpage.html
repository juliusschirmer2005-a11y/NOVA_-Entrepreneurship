Three real cases that happen in offices like yours

Illustrative scenarios based on common fraud patterns described by CNCS — names and amounts are invented

1

The Changed Bank Details

A long-time supplier "writes" that their bank account has changed. The invoice looks 100% authentic. The transfer is signed off, and client money goes directly to the attacker.

2

The Urgent Partner

An email pretending to be the senior partner: "I'm in a closing meeting, please make this €8,400 transfer today for a client deed and keep it between us."

3

The Shared Document

"A client shared a document with you." The link leads to a fake Office 365 login screen. Once typed, the attacker silently reads all incoming client emails for months.

Core Security Habits

The Five PAUSA Rules

Everything in our programme leads back to these five practical daily habits. Every employee receives them as a physical card for their desk.

1

Pause.

If an email is urgent, unexpected, or asks for money or a password: stop for ten seconds.

Defeats emotional pressure tactics.
2

Check the address, not name.

Anyone can type "Dr. Silva". Always inspect the actual email domain address.

Exposes spoofed identities.
3

Confirm payments by phone.

Bank details change ONLY after a phone call to a known number on file, never from the email.

Stops most invoice fraud.
4

Never log in from a link.

Type website addresses directly or use your saved browser bookmarks.

Protects mailbox passwords.
5

Report, don't delete.

Forward suspicious messages to your office contact. Nobody is blamed for reporting.

Warns colleagues in minutes.
